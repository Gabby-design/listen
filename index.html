The Story & Philosophy

Why I Built Listen

A personal journey from keyboard fatigue and broken speech tools to frictionless thought-to-text.

⚡

Thinking at 150 WPM vs. Typing at 40 WPM

The human mind thinks and speaks at 150+ words per minute. But when we type, our fingers bottleneck us down to 40 or 50 words per minute. Brilliant creative flow evaporates in the split-second it takes to reach for the backspace key, correct typos, or wrestle with keyboard fatigue. I built Listen because our tools should keep up with our minds—not slow them down.

🧠

Why Traditional Voice Dictation Failed

Every existing dictation tool I tried felt rigid, robotic, and dumb. If you say "number one clean the room", they spell out "number 1" in awkward spots instead of creating a numbered list. They have no idea whether "one" is a digit or a word in conversation. They cut you off if you pause for breath, and they ignore natural grammar and brackets. Listen solves this with a real two-stage intelligence engine: acoustic capture powered by Whisper, fused with an ultra-fast LLM context brain that understands human intent.

🔮

The Invisible Companion (Zero Focus Stealing)

I didn't want another bloated app window stealing focus from my code editor, browser, or chat window. Listen was designed to feel like an invisible superpower: a glowing neon fluid orb that appears silently at the bottom of your screen while you speak, and melts away the moment you stop. Your words are pasted straight into whatever app your cursor was already focused on.

⏱️

Deep Listening for Extended Focus

Whether dictating a quick 3-second reply in Slack or speaking for an entire hour to draft a book chapter, technical design doc, or essay, Listen does not lose your train of thought. It records with lightweight Opus compression and passes long audio streams seamlessly, understanding when to place periods, commas, question marks, and paragraphs without manual intervention.
