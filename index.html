Origin & Team

Who Built Listen?

Crafted by Gabriel (Gabby) and independent software engineers obsessed with input bandwidth and user ergonomics.

👨‍💻

The Maker's Story

Listen was conceived and built by Gabriel (Gabby)—a developer and writer tired of physical keyboard fatigue and the clumsy unreliability of existing speech tools. Every day, hours were lost stopping to correct typos, re-typing sentences, or waiting for bloated voice applications to respond. Gabriel engineered Listen to be the tool he needed himself: lightning fast, completely unobtrusive, and genuinely smart enough to understand natural speech rhythm.

🎯

Our Mission & Philosophy

We believe that typing should never be the bottleneck of human intellect. The mechanical keyboard was invented in 1868—over 150 years ago. While computing hardware and AI have leaped forward exponentially, our primary interface for getting ideas into machines has remained practically unchanged. Listen exists to bridge that gap forever.

The Philosophy

Why Listen Was Built

The fundamental flaws of traditional dictation tools—and how Listen solves them in writing.

⌨️

The 150 WPM vs 40 WPM Bottleneck

Human vocal cords and thought processes effortlessly produce 120 to 160 words per minute. Yet average typing speed hovers between 35 and 50 WPM. That means approximately 70% of your cognitive velocity is lost waiting for your fingers to hit mechanical switches. Ideas get lost, arguments lose their natural cadence, and mental stamina drains.

🤖

Why Legacy Speech Tools Failed

Traditional speech-to-text engines are rigid, unforgiving, and literal. When you say "number one clean the room then step two buy milk", legacy tools awkwardly spell out "number 1 clean the room then step 2 buy milk". They cannot distinguish when "one" is a digit or conversational English. They don't place question marks when you ask a question, they ignore spoken brackets, and they drop words if you pause for breath.

👻

The Problem with Window Bloat

Other dictation apps force you to open a heavy desktop window that steals your keyboard focus, interrupts your visual layout, and requires manual copy-pasting. Listen was built to be an invisible ghost: a borderless, organic fluid orb that hovers at your screen edge only when active, never stealing active cursor focus.

🔒

Developer-Managed Simplicity

We believe everyday users shouldn't have to navigate confusing cloud developer consoles, set up credit cards, or generate API keys just to dictate notes. Listen comes pre-configured and managed by developers out of the box. Install the setup wizard, select your preferred shortcut, and start dictating immediately.

Technical Architecture

How the Technology Works

A detailed architectural breakdown of the dual-intelligence pipeline and native OS integration.

1

Stage 1: Acoustic Whisper Capture

When you press your global shortcut, Listen activates high-clarity 16kHz mono audio recording using lightweight Opus compression. The stream is sent to Groq's LPUs running Whisper Large-v3-Turbo, capturing every syllable and word with acoustic precision in ~300ms.

2

Stage 2: Contextual LLM Reasoning

Raw speech is immediately piped to an ultra-fast LLM context model. The model analyzes grammatical structure, formats lists (e.g. converting "number one" into 1.), inserts commas and question marks, capitalizes names, and formats brackets in ~150ms.

3

Stage 3: Zero-Focus Fluid Orb

During speech, a borderless, transparent floating orb animates fluidly above your Windows taskbar. Engineered with non-focusable OS flags, it stays on top visually without ever stealing cursor focus away from your active software.

4

Stage 4: Native Keystroke Injection

Once formatted, Listen writes the polished transcription to the system clipboard and immediately executes native OS keystroke simulation to paste the text directly into the exact coordinates of your blinking cursor.

Extended 1-Hour Continuous Dictation

By streaming 32kbps mono Opus audio with 1-second chunks, an entire 60-minute dictation session consumes only ~14.4 MB of bandwidth—comfortably below the 25 MB payload ceiling. You can talk for an hour without losing a single thought.
